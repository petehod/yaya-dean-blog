Becoming a software engineer changed the way I think about music. In software development, there are data structures called objects, which can be thought of as a collection of
key and value pairs. These keys and value pairs are wrapped inside of `{}` (curly braces) and look something like this:

<CodeBlock caption="Here are some details about me represented as an object. Name, age, and occupation are the keys and Dean, 25, and Software Engineer are the values.">
    ```ts
    {
        name: "Dean",
        age: 25,
        occupation: "Software Engineer"
    }
    ```
</CodeBlock>

What I realized is that _songs_ can be thought of as objects. There are a set of keys that describe a song, and each key has a value. Here is an example of a song represented as an object:

<CodeBlock caption={`"Baby" by Justin Bieber represented as an object`}>
    ```ts
    {
        song_name: "Baby",
        artist: "Justin Bieber",
        featured_artist: "Ludacris",
        tempo: 130,
        key: "Eb Major",
        chord_progression: "Eb, C-, Ab, Bb",
        time_signature: "4/4",
        melody: ?? 
        // There are many more key/value pairs that could be added. 
        // This is just a small subset.
    }
    ```
</CodeBlock>

## Automating the Process

In software development, efficiency is key. One of the most valuable lessons I’ve learned as a software engineer is the importance of automating repetitive tasks.
By automating what can be automated, we free up our mental bandwidth for more complex and creative challenges.

When I approach music production and writing guitar loops, I apply the same principle. Many aspects of a song—like tempo, key, chord progressions, and scales—can be predetermined. By setting
these parameters in advance, I create a sort of “assembly line” for producing guitar loops. This approach allows me to focus on the parts of music that truly require human creativity, like crafting melodies
and playing those melodies with emotion.

For example, before I even touch my guitar, I’ll decide on the tempo, key, and basic chord progression. This is like setting up a skeleton or framework that
I can build upon. I'll even automate things as simple as coming up with the name for my file.

## The Art of Melody

In software development, most things can be reduced to a simple algorithm. Music, in many ways, is the same but there are elements of music that are hard to quanitfy, such as melody or dynamics.
For one, melodies can be copyrighted (although even that is up for debate. See this article on <a
style={{ textDecoration: "underline" }}
target="\_blank"
href="https://www.independent.co.uk/tech/music-copyright-algorithm-lawsuit-damien-riehl-a9364536.html"> Independent</a>).
But more importantly, melody is where we can deploy things that are hard to quanitfy, like emotion and feeling. These factors are called _dynamics_, and they are what take a song/melody from
good to great. Dynamics, in my opinion are the most important part of music, and I try to spend as much focus as possible on these elements.

## Speeding Up Production

By automating the more predictable elements of a song, I’m able to speed up the production process significantly. This “assembly line” approach allows me to pound out melodies faster than I ever could
before. I no longer have to spend 30+ minutes deciding on a chord progression or figuring out the right key. Instead, I can focus on what really matters: making the melody as compelling as possible and playing
it with the best dynamics I can.

Loops used to take me an hour to make, but when I started automating the process, I am able to make the loops in 5-10 minutes.

<Spacer size={"md"} />

## Conclusion

Learning software development has fundamentally changed the way I approach music. It’s taught me to value efficiency and to focus my creative energy on the aspects of music that can’t be automated.
By setting up an “assembly line” for guitar loop production, I’m able to streamline the process and produce music more quickly, all while ensuring that the most important part—the melody—gets the
attention it deserves.
